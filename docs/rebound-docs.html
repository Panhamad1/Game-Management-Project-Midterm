1. What is REBOUND?

REBOUND is a single-player, first-person physics puzzle and movement game. The player wakes up in a compact testing facility and must reach the exit door of each room. Rather than fighting enemies or shooting weapons, the player manipulates objects and their own momentum using three abilities:

Ability Physical Input Core Mechanic & Physics Action Gameplay Role
Left Click: Push (Wind Blast) Click Left Mouse Button (LMB) Shoots a compressed air blast that shoves objects forward, and kicks the player backward with 3D rocket recoil. Pushes blocks onto buttons, launches the player across pits, and deflects incoming enemy rockets.
Right Click: Pull (Vacuum Vortex) Hold Right Mouse Button (RMB) Creates a suction vortex that reels in crates or projectiles, hovering them 2.5 meters in front of your camera. Retrieves distant blocks, catches enemy grenades, and carries blocks to throw or stack.
Both Hands: Crate Cannon LMB while holding RMB Instantly releases the vacuum lock and fires the carried block forward as a high-speed projectile. Breaks barricades, crushes heavy armored enemies, and solves long-distance throwing puzzles.
Eyes: Slow Motion Hold Left Shift Slows down the world to 25% speed while keeping your run, look, and aim at normal full speed. Gives extra time to time mid-air rocket jumps, catch enemy rockets, and dodge moving hazards.

2. The Core Gameplay Loop

Every room in REBOUND is a focused puzzle. The game deliberately avoids wandering in open landscapes or listening to long dialogue scenes.

The 6-Step Player Loop

  1. Enter Room: The player steps into the chamber; the exit door and the physical obstacle are immediately visible.
  2. Inspect: The player identifies interactable objects (crates, barriers, switches) via visual glow highlights.
  3. Experiment: The player tests pull, push, recoil boosts, or slow motion in a safe starting area.
  4. Execute: The player chains abilities together (e.g., pulling a platform while pushing into empty air to jump a gap).
  5. Reach Exit: The player triggers the exit sensor, opening the door to the next stage.
  6. Progress: The game loads the next room with a new, slightly harder variation of the mechanics.

Core Design Guardrails

  • One Lesson Per Room: Never introduce two unfamiliar mechanics in the same room.
  • Zero Invisible Rules: Solutions must rely on visible physics, not hidden triggers or secret buttons.
  • Predictable Recoil: Pushing empty air produces the exact same backwards kick as pushing a wall or box.
  • Fast Recovery: Falling into a pit resets the player quickly at the start of that room without reloading the entire game.
  • Independent Hands: Left hand and right hand operate simultaneously on two separate objects.

3. Architectural Decision Records (ADRs)

An Architectural Decision Record (ADR) documents an important engineering choice, why it was chosen, and what trade-offs were accepted. Below are the five foundational ADRs for REBOUND:

Decision ID Architecture Decision Why This Was Chosen (Plain English) Accepted Trade-Off & Risk Mitigation
ADR-01 Rigidbody Player Controller Unity's built-in CharacterController component does not naturally respond to physics forces, impulses, or explosions. Because the push recoil mechanic pushes the player backwards through physics impulses, a Rigidbody player controller is required. Rigidbody characters can tip over or snag on floor seams. Mitigation: Freeze X and Z rotations in Rigidbody constraints, and use a smooth capsule collider with continuous collision detection.
ADR-02 Predictable, Flat Recoil Amount In real physics, pushback depends on target mass and distance. In REBOUND, push recoil gives a fixed 5.0 impulse units whether the player hits a heavy block, a light box, or empty space. This makes jumping and air-movement predictable for the player to master. The game breaks real-world Newton physics intentionally to prioritize fun and predictable platforming.
ADR-03 Continuous Physics Force Pull (No Childing) When pulling an object, the script applies an accelerating physics force toward a point in front of the camera. The object is never made a child of the camera transform. Childing breaks physics collisions and causes objects to phase through walls. Objects can wobble or overshoot if force is unchecked. Mitigation: Add velocity damping, a maximum speed clamp, and a 0.2m dead zone around the target point.
ADR-04 Single Target Per Hand & Target Lock Each hand can only control one object at a time. The left hand cannot pull the exact same object that the right hand is pushing in the same frame. A target lock on the object prevents conflicting physics forces. Players cannot multi-grab piles of items. This keeps physics calculations clean and stops erratic jitter.
ADR-05 Greybox-First Development All levels and mechanics must be built and proven using primitive gray boxes and prototype materials before 3D models or art assets are imported. If a room is not fun as gray boxes, visual art will not fix it. Graphics look simple during early development, but team time is protected against wasted art rework.
ADR-06 Momentum Sliding & Recoil-Convert Boost To capture fast Redliner-style flow, crouching while moving triggers a momentum slide that accelerates down slopes and preserves horizontal speed through bunny-hops. Firing Push while sliding redirects explosive recoil into forward slide velocity rather than backward kick. High velocities (up to 35 m/s) risk collision tunneling. Mitigation: ContinuousDynamic CCD on player and props, thick level colliders, overhead raycast lockouts, and gentle aerodynamic drag above 35 m/s.
ADR-07 Dual-Mode Pull (Grapple Slingshot vs Prop Snatch) Instead of giving the player conventional guns, Pull serves as both high-speed traversal (grappling walls/anchors with slingshot release) and weaponized object snatching (grabbing outline-highlighted crates and light enemies to fire as kinetic projectiles). Potential targeting confusion when an object sits in front of a wall. Mitigation: Target-under-crosshair priority hierarchy and elastic retraction in narrow vents to prevent prop snagging.
ADR-08 Momentum-Siphon Focus & Telegraphed Combat Slow-motion cannot be spammed passively; it siphons energy exclusively from high-speed movement and target impacts. Enemies challenge fast players through 1.0s predictive telegraph lasers and tripwires rather than unfair hitscan. Turret AI requires velocity-lead prediction algorithms and telegraph line renderers, balancing skill-expression at top speeds.
ADR-09 4-Tier Enemy System & Player Spider Sense To balance high player mobility and gunless combat, enemies are built around 4 distinct mechanical profiles: Swarm Rushers (high-speed bowling), Hulk Leapers (leap slam + back weak-point), Snipers (1.5s lock + bullet deflection), and Rapid Sentries (suppression vs crate riot shield). A directional Spider Sense flashes on HUD right before lethal attacks, offering an instant Slow-Mo reflex window. Requires synchronized threat tracking between enemy AI scripts and the player's HUD, plus clear visual telegraphs to prevent feeling unfair.

4. Project Scope Boundaries

Scope discipline is critical for a student game. The team must stay focused on what makes REBOUND fun and eliminate non-essential features:

Strictly In Scope (Must Build)

  • First-person movement (WASD, mouse look, jump, air control).
  • High-speed momentum slide, slope acceleration, bunny-hop speed preservation, and recoil-convert boost.
  • Dual-mode Pull: Wall/anchor grapple slingshot AND illuminated object/light enemy snatching.
  • Crate Cannon kinetic projectile launch (smashing barricades, switches, and armor-crushing enemies).
  • Momentum-siphon slow-motion meter with unscaled camera aim and floaty air control.
  • Dynamic Speed Camera Controller (FOV 85-105, camera banking into turns, wind audio crescendo).
  • Enemy AI integration via IKineticTarget: Swarm Crawlers (bowling ram), Hulk Leapers (shockwave slam + back core), Snipers (1.5s lock + bullet deflect), Rapid Laser Sentries (crate riot shield), and Laser Tripwires.
  • Player Threat Sense ("Spider Sense") directional HUD warning arc with instant Slow-Mo reflex trigger.
  • Continuous Dynamic CCD, overhead clearance raycast, and 35 m/s soft speed cap.
  • One dedicated Prototype Lab scene for physics, movement, and combat testing.
  • A progression of 5 to 7 high-speed time-trial parkour courses with split times and medal targets.
  • Basic UI: Crosshair with target highlight glow, speedrun medal timer, slow-motion meter, pause menu.
  • Simple audio cues: wind blast, suction hum, slow-mo chime, impact thuds.

Strictly Out of Scope (Forbidden / Cut)

  • No Complex Gun Inventories: No ammo counters, weapon wheels, or conventional firearms (air powers only).
  • No Story Cutscenes: No character dialogue, voice actors, or scripted cinematics.
  • No Open World: No sprawling maps, terrain systems, or outdoor hubs.
  • No Complex Destruction: No mesh fracturing or real-time breaking glass.
  • No Inventory or Crafting: No bag management or item combining.
  • No Multiplayer: Single-player offline only.
  • No Character Customization: No skill trees, stat upgrades, or skins.

Edge Cases, Safeguards & Combat Balance Rules

Edge Case / State Potential Failure Mode Implemented Architecture Solution
Spider-Sense Threat Stacking Multiple snipers or Hulk slams happen at the same time, confusing HUD chevrons. Priority & Multi-Arc Clamping: The closest immediate lethal attack flashes with highest opacity; secondary threats display smaller faint chevrons.
Sniper Reflection Alignment Player Push deflection fires in a random direction and misses the sniper. Laser-Trace Reversal: The deflected bullet calculates its return trajectory along the sniper's own targeting ray, guaranteeing a direct hit if deflected cleanly.
Hulk Ground Slam Jitter Player is near the slam edge and gets stuck in physics jitter between capsule and wave. Trigger Volume Sphere-Cast: Shockwave is evaluated as an expanding radial trigger volume with clear 1.5m height check (jump/grapple clears).
Swarmer Ramming at Low Speed Player walks into a Swarmer pack at 3 m/s expecting a bowling strike. Speed Threshold (12 m/s): Bowling strike only triggers at slide/grapple speed (>=12 m/s). Low-speed collisions deal contact damage to player.
Carrying Props in Tight Corridors A crate held 2.5m ahead jams into doorframes or wedges player against corners. Elastic Retraction: Proximity sensors retract the hover anchor from 2.5m to 1.2m when walls are detected, smoothing narrow corridor navigation.
Wall Collision at 35 m/s Player or thrown crate tunnels through thin walls or floor meshes. ContinuousDynamic CCD: Rigidbodies evaluate sweep volumes every sub-step; static level walls are modeled with minimum 0.5m thickness.
Un-Crouch Under Low Obstacles Player releases Crouch inside a low vent, clipping head into geometry. Overhead Clearance Raycast: Checks Physics.Raycast(head, Vector3.up, 1.0m). If blocked, crouch height remains locked at 0.9m until fully clear.
Target Ambiguity (Wall vs Crate) Player wants to grapple a wall behind an object, or grab a crate in front of a wall. Target-Priority Hierarchy: Layer 10 (Interactables) and Layer 12 (Light Enemies) take precedence if directly under the crosshair; otherwise falls back to Layer 9/14 (Grapple).

Level Architecture & Room Progression

A room-by-room blueprint for REBOUND's seven test chambers. Explains how each room teaches one clear mechanic using a reusable modular prefab kit.

Level Design Core Principles

REBOUND borrows the proven puzzle design philosophy seen in games like Portal 2: the environment itself teaches the player what to do. The player should never need a text box or voiceover explaining the solution.

Core Principles

  • Immediate Objective: When stepping into a room, the player must immediately spot the exit door and the obstacle in between.
  • One Lesson Per Chamber: Introduce only one new mechanic or combination per room.
  • Safe Play Area: Place movable objects in a safe starting area where the player can test their powers before risking a fall.
  • Instant Recovery: If the player misses a jump and falls into a hazard pit, teleport them back to the entrance immediately. Never force a full game restart.

Banned Level Anti-Patterns

  • No Invisible Rules: Never hide keys or triggers in obscure corners without visual clues.
  • No Blind Leaps: The destination platform must always be visible before jumping.
  • No Empty Corridors: Avoid long, boring hallways that add walking time without gameplay.
  • No Irreversible Softlocks: If an object falls into a pit, it must respawn automatically at its starting point.

The 7-Room Progression Matrix

The campaign is organized into seven sequential rooms that systematically build player skill:

ROOM 1 Learn Pull: Bridging the 3-Meter Gap Goal: Pull crate into chasm to walk across
ROOM 2 Learn Push: Activating the Floor Switch Goal: Blast crate onto floor plate to unlock exit
ROOM 3 Learn Recoil: The Rocket Jump Leap Goal: Boost across a 6m chasm using push recoil
ROOM 4 Learn Slow Motion: Timing the Speed Trap Goal: Slow down the sliding hazard to walk through safely
ROOM 5 Combine Hands: Simultaneous Dual Coordination Goal: Pull high crate with Left Hand while blasting gate with Right Hand
Room # Teaching Objective Room Layout & Physical Challenge Intended Player Action & Solution
Room 1 Learn Pull The player starts on a platform separated from the exit by a 3-metre gap. A movable crate sits on the far platform. Player aims at the crate, holds Right Click (RMB) to pull it into the gap, creating a bridge to walk across and exit.
Room 2 Learn Push The exit door is locked. A heavy floor pressure plate is visible to the side, and a medium crate rests in the center. Player positions themselves behind the crate, taps Left Click (LMB) to push it onto the pressure plate, triggering the door open.
Room 3 Learn Recoil A 6-metre gap separates the player from the exit ledge. The gap is too wide for a standard jump. No movable objects exist in the room. Player sprints forward, jumps into the air, and taps Left Click (LMB) aiming backward/downward. The recoil impulse boosts the player across the chasm.
Room 4 Learn Slow Motion A fast hydraulic barrier slides rapidly across the walkway every 0.6 seconds, knocking anyone who steps through into a reset pit. Player holds Left Shift to slow time by 75%. The barrier slows down noticeably, allowing the player to walk safely past during the opening.
Room 5 Combine Pull & Push A heavy barrier blocks the doorway, while a crate sits on a high ledge. The player must clear the door and weigh down a switch simultaneously. Player holds Right Click (RMB) to pull the crate off the ledge, while simultaneously tapping Left Click (LMB) to blast the barrier away or launch the crate.
Room 6 Combine All Abilities A moving platform oscillates across a vast pit. An essential crate is falling from a ceiling chute. Player activates Slow Motion, catches the falling crate with Pull, leaps toward the moving platform, and uses Push Recoil to ensure a safe landing.
Room 7 Final Mastery Chamber A multi-stage test chamber featuring a timed moving platform, two weighted switches, and a high exit ledge requiring recoil jumping. Mastery test: Pulling crates onto switches, timing slow-motion runs across moving platforms, and executing an aerial recoil boost to reach the final exit.

Room Design Evaluation Checklist

Before adding a room to the official build, the level designer must test it against these ten questions:

  1. Is the exit door visible from the room start? (If not, add overhead lighting or directional signs).
  2. Can the player clearly tell which objects are movable? (Verify all interactables have emissive trim or colored materials).
  3. Does the room teach only one primary concept? (Avoid combining two new mechanics in the same room).
  4. Can the player experiment safely before attempting a risky maneuver?
  5. If an object drops into a pit, does it respawn automatically?
  6. Does falling into a pit reset the player cleanly within 1 second?
  7. Can the room be solved without exploiting glitches?
  8. Is the intended jump distance comfortable with a standard recoil blast?
  9. Does slow motion provide an obvious, tangible advantage?
  10. Has someone who did not build the room completed it without verbal hints?

4. Version Control Hygiene & Unity Git Rules

Unity projects create thousands of temporary cache files every time a scene is opened. Never commit Unity's generated cache files to Git. Doing so inflates the repository size to gigabytes and causes impossible merge conflicts.

Folders That MUST Be Committed (Source of Truth)

  • Assets/ — All scripts, prefabs, materials, and scenes created by the team.
  • Packages/manifest.json — Unity Package Manager dependency list.
  • ProjectSettings/ — Physics layers, input actions, tags, and editor configurations.
  • .gitignore — Root repository exclusion rules.

Folders That MUST Be Ignored (Never Commit)

  • Library/ — Unity's local asset cache (regenerated automatically on each computer).
  • Temp/ — Runtime temporary build files.
  • Obj/ & Build/ — Compiled C# assemblies and standalone game builds.
  • Logs/ & MemoryCaptures/ — Editor diagnostic output.
  • .vs/ & .idea/ — Visual Studio / Rider IDE user settings.
Gold Standard Team Collaboration Rule:

Never allow two team members to edit the same .unity scene file at the same time. Unity scene files are massive YAML files that cannot be merged with Git. Instead, work inside separate scenes (e.g. Person 1 in PrototypeLab, Person 3 in Level_01), and package all gameplay objects into Prefabs before sharing them.

3. Technical Risk Mitigation Matrix

Identified risks from OPEN_QUESTIONS_AND_RISKS.md and PHYSICS_AND_UNITY_NOTES.md paired with concrete preventative solutions:

Risk ID Severity Failure Mode Description Empirical Engineering Solution
RISK-01 Critical Slow motion also slows player movement: Reducing Time.timeScale causes the player Rigidbody to move at 25% speed, defeating the entire gameplay purpose. Solution: When slow motion triggers, multiply player movement force and jump impulse by (1.0f / Time.timeScale) to compensate, and calculate camera look using Time.unscaledDeltaTime. If global time remains unstable, switch to per-object time control on puzzle entities.
RISK-02 Critical Objects clipping through walls when pulled: High pull acceleration causes crates to tunnel through thin wall colliders. Solution: Enable CollisionDetectionMode.Continuous on all interactable crates. Enforce an 8.0 m/s maximum velocity clamp in PullAbility.FixedUpdate() and use thick (0.5m+) wall colliders.
RISK-03 High Both hands grabbing the same object: Left hand pulls while right hand pushes, causing opposing impulses that crash or violently vibrate the object. Solution: Enforce a target lock in AbilityTarget.cs: if IsControlled == true, reject interactions from the other hand until released.
RISK-04 High Player recoil tipping capsule over: Powerful upward or downward push recoil causes the Rigidbody capsule to fall over like a bowling pin. Solution: Check Freeze Rotation X and Freeze Rotation Z in the Rigidbody inspector. Rotate the camera pivot independently for pitch.
RISK-05 Medium Git merge conflicts in scene files: Two team members work on the same scene simultaneously, corrupting Unity's YAML serialization. Solution: Strict rule: only one person edits a given .unity scene file at a time. All reusable assets, triggers, and level blocks must be built as Prefabs.
RISK-06 Medium Irreversible puzzle softlocks: An essential crate falls off a ledge into an unreachable pit, making the room impossible to complete. Solution: Attach an auto-respawner trigger in every hazard pit that resets dropped puzzle objects back to their initial spawn transform.
