Level Architecture & Room Progression

A room-by-room blueprint for REBOUND's seven test chambers. Explains how each room teaches one clear mechanic using a reusable modular prefab kit.

Level Design Core Principles

REBOUND borrows the proven puzzle design philosophy seen in games like Portal 2: the environment itself teaches the player what to do. The player should never need a text box or voiceover explaining the solution.

Core Principles

  • Immediate Objective: When stepping into a room, the player must immediately spot the exit door and the obstacle in between.
  • One Lesson Per Chamber: Introduce only one new mechanic or combination per room.
  • Safe Play Area: Place movable objects in a safe starting area where the player can test their powers before risking a fall.
  • Instant Recovery: If the player misses a jump and falls into a hazard pit, teleport them back to the entrance immediately. Never force a full game restart.

Banned Level Anti-Patterns

  • No Invisible Rules: Never hide keys or triggers in obscure corners without visual clues.
  • No Blind Leaps: The destination platform must always be visible before jumping.
  • No Empty Corridors: Avoid long, boring hallways that add walking time without gameplay.
  • No Irreversible Softlocks: If an object falls into a pit, it must respawn automatically at its starting point.

The 7-Room Progression Matrix

The campaign is organized into seven sequential rooms that systematically build player skill:

ROOM 1 Learn Pull: Bridging the 3-Meter Gap Goal: Pull crate into chasm to walk across
ROOM 2 Learn Push: Activating the Floor Switch Goal: Blast crate onto floor plate to unlock exit
ROOM 3 Learn Recoil: The Rocket Jump Leap Goal: Boost across a 6m chasm using push recoil
ROOM 4 Learn Slow Motion: Timing the Speed Trap Goal: Slow down the sliding hazard to walk through safely
ROOM 5 Combine Hands: Simultaneous Dual Coordination Goal: Pull high crate with Left Hand while blasting gate with Right Hand
Room # Teaching Objective Room Layout & Physical Challenge Intended Player Action & Solution
Room 1 Learn Pull The player starts on a platform separated from the exit by a 3-metre gap. A movable crate sits on the far platform. Player aims at the crate, holds Right Click (RMB) to pull it into the gap, creating a bridge to walk across and exit.
Room 2 Learn Push The exit door is locked. A heavy floor pressure plate is visible to the side, and a medium crate rests in the center. Player positions themselves behind the crate, taps Left Click (LMB) to push it onto the pressure plate, triggering the door open.
Room 3 Learn Recoil A 6-metre gap separates the player from the exit ledge. The gap is too wide for a standard jump. No movable objects exist in the room. Player sprints forward, jumps into the air, and taps Left Click (LMB) aiming backward/downward. The recoil impulse boosts the player across the chasm.
Room 4 Learn Slow Motion A fast hydraulic barrier slides rapidly across the walkway every 0.6 seconds, knocking anyone who steps through into a reset pit. Player holds Left Shift to slow time by 75%. The barrier slows down noticeably, allowing the player to walk safely past during the opening.
Room 5 Combine Pull & Push A heavy barrier blocks the doorway, while a crate sits on a high ledge. The player must clear the door and weigh down a switch simultaneously. Player holds Right Click (RMB) to pull the crate off the ledge, while simultaneously tapping Left Click (LMB) to blast the barrier away or launch the crate.
Room 6 Combine All Abilities A moving platform oscillates across a vast pit. An essential crate is falling from a ceiling chute. Player activates Slow Motion, catches the falling crate with Pull, leaps toward the moving platform, and uses Push Recoil to ensure a safe landing.
Room 7 Final Mastery Chamber A multi-stage test chamber featuring a timed moving platform, two weighted switches, and a high exit ledge requiring recoil jumping. Mastery test: Pulling crates onto switches, timing slow-motion runs across moving platforms, and executing an aerial recoil boost to reach the final exit.

Room Design Evaluation Checklist

Before adding a room to the official build, the level designer must test it against these ten questions:

  1. Is the exit door visible from the room start? (If not, add overhead lighting or directional signs).
  2. Can the player clearly tell which objects are movable? (Verify all interactables have emissive trim or colored materials).
  3. Does the room teach only one primary concept? (Avoid combining two new mechanics in the same room).
  4. Can the player experiment safely before attempting a risky maneuver?
  5. If an object drops into a pit, does it respawn automatically?
  6. Does falling into a pit reset the player cleanly within 1 second?
  7. Can the room be solved without exploiting glitches?
  8. Is the intended jump distance comfortable with a standard recoil blast?
  9. Does slow motion provide an obvious, tangible advantage?
  10. Has someone who did not build the room completed it without verbal hints?
