System Architecture & Script Contracts

A simple breakdown of how REBOUND works in Unity. Explains how the player, abilities, physics, enemies, and rooms connect without messy bugs or tangled scripts.

The Four Simple Code Layers

The code is organized into four clean steps. Input flows down into ability math; ability math moves physics bodies; physics bodies trigger puzzle switches and hurt enemies. Zoom and pan the diagram below:

Player Prefab Structure (PlayerRoot)

The player is built as a nested prefab. Physics movement belongs on the body capsule; looking and abilities belong on the camera pivot. This stops the camera from tumbling when you get hit by push recoil:

Physics Layer Setup & Collision Matrix

Setting up Unity Physics layers correctly prevents glitches (like raycasts hitting your own face) and keeps performance high:

Layer Name What Belongs on This Layer Behavior with Player Abilities
Layer 8: Player PlayerRoot capsule collider and ground sensors. Collides with Environment, Blocks, and Enemies. Ignored by ability rays so you never target yourself.
Layer 9: Environment Static floors, walls, ceilings, and ramps. Collides with everything. Blocks ability rays (cannot pull objects through solid walls).
Layer 10: Interactables Crates, puzzle blocks, and physics props. Targeted by Wind Blast and Vacuum Vortex. Collides safely with player.
Layer 11: Hazards Bottomless pits and reset triggers. Trigger only. Detects player fall to restart room.
Layer 12: Enemies Enemy bodies and hurtboxes. Targeted by wind blasts (Push) and thrown crates (Crush). Small swarms can be pulled.
Layer 13: Projectiles Incoming enemy rockets, grenades, and plasma orbs. Can be deflected with Wind Blast (Push) or caught with Vacuum Vortex (Pull).
