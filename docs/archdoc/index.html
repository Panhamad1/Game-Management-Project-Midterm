1. What is REBOUND?

REBOUND is a single-player, first-person physics puzzle and movement game. The player wakes up in a compact testing facility and must reach the exit door of each room. Rather than fighting enemies or shooting weapons, the player manipulates objects and their own momentum using three abilities:

Ability Physical Input Core Mechanic & Physics Action Gameplay Role
Left Click: Push & Deflect Left Mouse Button (LMB) Shoots a kinetic air cannon blast that kicks the player backward with rocket recoil, launches carried props at 32 m/s, and deflects enemy plasma bolts within 3.8m. Pushes Egyptian props onto switches, launches the player across chasms, deflects sniper/sentry bullets back at enemies (+20 Focus), and breaks barricades.
Right Click: Pull & Carry Hold Right Mouse Button (RMB) Creates a focused kinetic tractor beam (18m range) that reels in props and seats them stably in the bottom-right carry pocket (0.42m, -0.32m, 1.35m). Picks up Scarab Urns, Sandstone Boulders, and Obsidian Slabs without blocking your forward crosshair view.
Both Hands: Kinetic Prop Cannon LMB while holding RMB Instantly releases the tractor lock and fires the carried prop forward at 32 m/s as a heavy kinetic missile. Activates Ankh target switches, staggers Hulk Leapers, crushes Swarmer packs with physical knockback, and breaks stone walls.
Key Z: Slow Motion Focus Press or Hold Z Key Slows down world physics to 25% speed while keeping your run, look, aim, and reactions at 100% full speed. Center screen stays crisp with subtle 12px edge borders. Gives reaction time to line up mid-air jumps, deflect incoming sniper bolts, and dodge Hulk ground shockwaves.

2. The Core Gameplay Loop

Every room in REBOUND is a focused puzzle. The game deliberately avoids wandering in open landscapes or listening to long dialogue scenes.

The 6-Step Player Loop

  1. Enter Room: The player steps into the chamber; the exit door and the physical obstacle are immediately visible.
  2. Inspect: The player identifies interactable objects (crates, barriers, switches) via visual glow highlights.
  3. Experiment: The player tests pull, push, recoil boosts, or slow motion in a safe starting area.
  4. Execute: The player chains abilities together (e.g., pulling a platform while pushing into empty air to jump a gap).
  5. Reach Exit: The player triggers the exit sensor, opening the door to the next stage.
  6. Progress: The game loads the next room with a new, slightly harder variation of the mechanics.

Core Design Guardrails

  • One Lesson Per Room: Never introduce two unfamiliar mechanics in the same room.
  • Zero Invisible Rules: Solutions must rely on visible physics, not hidden triggers or secret buttons.
  • Predictable Recoil: Pushing empty air produces the exact same backwards kick as pushing a wall or box.
  • Fast Recovery: Falling into a pit resets the player quickly at the start of that room without reloading the entire game.
  • Independent Hands: Left hand and right hand operate simultaneously on two separate objects.

3. Architectural Decision Records (ADRs)

An Architectural Decision Record (ADR) documents an important engineering choice, why it was chosen, and what trade-offs were accepted. Below are the five foundational ADRs for REBOUND:

Decision ID Architecture Decision Why This Was Chosen (Plain English) Accepted Trade-Off & Risk Mitigation
ADR-01 Rigidbody Player Controller Unity's built-in CharacterController component does not naturally respond to physics forces, impulses, or explosions. Because the push recoil mechanic pushes the player backwards through physics impulses, a Rigidbody player controller is required. Rigidbody characters can tip over or snag on floor seams. Mitigation: Freeze X and Z rotations in Rigidbody constraints, and use a smooth capsule collider with continuous collision detection.
ADR-02 Predictable, Flat Recoil Amount In real physics, pushback depends on target mass and distance. In REBOUND, push recoil gives a fixed 5.0 impulse units whether the player hits a heavy block, a light box, or empty space. This makes jumping and air-movement predictable for the player to master. The game breaks real-world Newton physics intentionally to prioritize fun and predictable platforming.
ADR-03 Continuous Physics Force Pull (No Childing) When pulling an object, the script applies an accelerating physics force toward a point in front of the camera. The object is never made a child of the camera transform. Childing breaks physics collisions and causes objects to phase through walls. Objects can wobble or overshoot if force is unchecked. Mitigation: Add velocity damping, a maximum speed clamp, and a 0.2m dead zone around the target point.
ADR-04 Single Target Per Hand & Target Lock Each hand can only control one object at a time. The left hand cannot pull the exact same object that the right hand is pushing in the same frame. A target lock on the object prevents conflicting physics forces. Players cannot multi-grab piles of items. This keeps physics calculations clean and stops erratic jitter.
ADR-05 Greybox-First Development All levels and mechanics must be built and proven using primitive gray boxes and prototype materials before 3D models or art assets are imported. If a room is not fun as gray boxes, visual art will not fix it. Graphics look simple during early development, but team time is protected against wasted art rework.
ADR-06 Calibrated Locomotion & Parkour State Machine Walking is set to 3.2 m/s, sprint to 5.4 m/s (Left Shift), and slide to 4.8 m/s (Ctrl) to give deliberate control. Parkour integrates a 1.5s wall-hang cling with vertical vaulting (+9.5m on Space) and directional zig-zag wall-jumping (10.5 m/s normal push + 8.0 m/s lift) with a clean 9.0 m/s maximum speed cap. Tight speed caps prevent collision tunneling through thin geometry and keep the player within human reaction limits during platforming.
ADR-07 Focused Kinetic Pull (Object Manipulation vs Grapple Removal) Grapple winch traversal was removed because pulling on walls bypassed parkour wall-running, ledge climbing, and puzzle geometry. Pull is strictly focused on picking up Egyptian props (Urns, Boulders, Slabs) into a dedicated bottom-right carry pocket (0.42m, -0.32m, 1.35m) so the forward screen view is unobstructed. Players cannot fly across maps on ropes, but gain dependable kinetic weaponry and clean visibility for combat.
ADR-08 Chrono-Dilation & Bullet Deflection Parry Pressing Z activates Slow Motion, setting physics time scale to 0.25 while keeping player aim and movement unscaled. Pushing (`LMB`) within a 3.8m parry cone deflects enemy plasma bolts back toward the attacker for 2x damage and restores +20 Focus energy. Requires precise collision sweeps in unscaled time to detect fast incoming projectiles without missing frames.
ADR-09 4-Tier Enemy System & Physical Reaction Feedback Enemies react physically to kinetic combat: Swarmers are flung across rooms and have a 65% chance of exploding on death; Hulk Leapers shake the camera during ground slams within 20m and stumble when struck by heavy props; Snipers track with telegraph lasers; and Sentries fire deflectable plasma bursts. Requires physical impulse and hit-flash integration on all enemy rigidbodies instead of static health reductions.

4. Project Scope Boundaries

Scope discipline is critical for a student game. The team must stay focused on what makes REBOUND fun and eliminate non-essential features:

Strictly In Scope (Must Build)

  • First-person parkour movement (WASD walk 3.2 m/s, Left Shift sprint 5.4 m/s, Ctrl slide 4.8 m/s, Q dash).
  • Wall-running, 1.5s wall-hang cling, Space wall-climb vault (+9.5m), and zig-zag wall-jumping.
  • Kinetic Push (`LMB`): Rocket recoil, 32 m/s prop launch, and 3.8m bullet deflection parry.
  • Kinetic Pull (`RMB`): 18m tractor beam that seats props in the bottom-right carry pocket without blocking view.
  • Chrono-Dilation Slow Motion (`Z` Key): 25% physics time scale, crisp screen with subtle 12px edge border accents.
  • Egyptian prop hierarchy with ambient auras: Scarab Jade Urns (1.5kg, mint), Sandstone Boulders (12kg, amber), Obsidian Slabs (45kg, ruby).
  • 4 enemy archetypes: Swarmers (65% explosive overload), Hulk Leapers (ground smash proximity shake), Sentries (rapid plasma), Snipers (laser lock).
  • The 180m, 6-Chamber Egyptian Parkour & Combat Tutorial Academy with checkpoint respawns and void kill plane.
  • Combat HUD: Dynamic reticle feedback, player and enemy health bars, floating damage numbers, and focus meter.
  • Full audio and visual feedback: Air blast, suction hum, deflection chime, and impact thuds.

Strictly Out of Scope (Forbidden / Cut)

  • No Grapple Traversal: No rope swinging, cable zip lines, or wall-winch traversal (parkour handles all movement).
  • No Complex Gun Inventories: No ammo counters, weapon wheels, or conventional firearms (kinetic powers and props only).
  • No Story Cutscenes: No character dialogue, voice actors, or scripted cinematics.
  • No Open World: No sprawling terrain, outdoor hubs, or procedural infinite levels.
  • No Complex Mesh Destruction: No real-time vertex fracturing or breaking glass.
  • No Inventory or Crafting: No backpacks, crafting tables, or item combining.
  • No Multiplayer: Single-player offline only.
  • No Open World: No sprawling maps, terrain systems, or outdoor hubs.
  • No Complex Destruction: No mesh fracturing or real-time breaking glass.
  • No Inventory or Crafting: No bag management or item combining.
  • No Multiplayer: Single-player offline only.
  • No Character Customization: No skill trees, stat upgrades, or skins.
