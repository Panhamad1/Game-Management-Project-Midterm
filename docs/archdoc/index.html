1. What is REBOUND?

REBOUND is a single-player, first-person physics puzzle and movement game. The player wakes up in a compact testing facility and must reach the exit door of each room. Rather than fighting enemies or shooting weapons, the player manipulates objects and their own momentum using three abilities:

Ability Physical Input Core Mechanic & Physics Action Gameplay Role
Left Click: Push (Wind Blast) Click Left Mouse Button (LMB) Shoots a compressed air blast that shoves objects forward, and kicks the player backward with 3D rocket recoil. Pushes blocks onto buttons, launches the player across pits, and deflects incoming enemy rockets.
Right Click: Pull (Vacuum Vortex) Hold Right Mouse Button (RMB) Creates a suction vortex that reels in crates or projectiles, hovering them 2.5 meters in front of your camera. Retrieves distant blocks, catches enemy grenades, and carries blocks to throw or stack.
Both Hands: Crate Cannon LMB while holding RMB Instantly releases the vacuum lock and fires the carried block forward as a high-speed projectile. Breaks barricades, crushes heavy armored enemies, and solves long-distance throwing puzzles.
Eyes: Slow Motion Hold Left Shift Slows down the world to 25% speed while keeping your run, look, and aim at normal full speed. Gives extra time to time mid-air rocket jumps, catch enemy rockets, and dodge moving hazards.

2. The Core Gameplay Loop

Every room in REBOUND is a focused puzzle. The game deliberately avoids wandering in open landscapes or listening to long dialogue scenes.

The 6-Step Player Loop

  1. Enter Room: The player steps into the chamber; the exit door and the physical obstacle are immediately visible.
  2. Inspect: The player identifies interactable objects (crates, barriers, switches) via visual glow highlights.
  3. Experiment: The player tests pull, push, recoil boosts, or slow motion in a safe starting area.
  4. Execute: The player chains abilities together (e.g., pulling a platform while pushing into empty air to jump a gap).
  5. Reach Exit: The player triggers the exit sensor, opening the door to the next stage.
  6. Progress: The game loads the next room with a new, slightly harder variation of the mechanics.

Core Design Guardrails

  • One Lesson Per Room: Never introduce two unfamiliar mechanics in the same room.
  • Zero Invisible Rules: Solutions must rely on visible physics, not hidden triggers or secret buttons.
  • Predictable Recoil: Pushing empty air produces the exact same backwards kick as pushing a wall or box.
  • Fast Recovery: Falling into a pit resets the player quickly at the start of that room without reloading the entire game.
  • Independent Hands: Left hand and right hand operate simultaneously on two separate objects.

3. Architectural Decision Records (ADRs)

An Architectural Decision Record (ADR) documents an important engineering choice, why it was chosen, and what trade-offs were accepted. Below are the five foundational ADRs for REBOUND:

Decision ID Architecture Decision Why This Was Chosen (Plain English) Accepted Trade-Off & Risk Mitigation
ADR-01 Rigidbody Player Controller Unity's built-in CharacterController component does not naturally respond to physics forces, impulses, or explosions. Because the push recoil mechanic pushes the player backwards through physics impulses, a Rigidbody player controller is required. Rigidbody characters can tip over or snag on floor seams. Mitigation: Freeze X and Z rotations in Rigidbody constraints, and use a smooth capsule collider with continuous collision detection.
ADR-02 Predictable, Flat Recoil Amount In real physics, pushback depends on target mass and distance. In REBOUND, push recoil gives a fixed 5.0 impulse units whether the player hits a heavy block, a light box, or empty space. This makes jumping and air-movement predictable for the player to master. The game breaks real-world Newton physics intentionally to prioritize fun and predictable platforming.
ADR-03 Continuous Physics Force Pull (No Childing) When pulling an object, the script applies an accelerating physics force toward a point in front of the camera. The object is never made a child of the camera transform. Childing breaks physics collisions and causes objects to phase through walls. Objects can wobble or overshoot if force is unchecked. Mitigation: Add velocity damping, a maximum speed clamp, and a 0.2m dead zone around the target point.
ADR-04 Single Target Per Hand & Target Lock Each hand can only control one object at a time. The left hand cannot pull the exact same object that the right hand is pushing in the same frame. A target lock on the object prevents conflicting physics forces. Players cannot multi-grab piles of items. This keeps physics calculations clean and stops erratic jitter.
ADR-05 Greybox-First Development All levels and mechanics must be built and proven using primitive gray boxes and prototype materials before 3D models or art assets are imported. If a room is not fun as gray boxes, visual art will not fix it. Graphics look simple during early development, but team time is protected against wasted art rework.

4. Project Scope Boundaries

Scope discipline is critical for a student game. The team must stay focused on what makes REBOUND fun and eliminate non-essential features:

Strictly In Scope (Must Build)

  • First-person movement (WASD, mouse look, jump, air control).
  • Left-hand continuous pull with physics damping.
  • Right-hand push impulse with player recoil (air-pushing supported).
  • Dual-hand simultaneous control on separate objects.
  • Crate Cannon combo launch and projectile deflection.
  • Slow-motion energy meter with floaty mid-air fall gravity.
  • Enemy AI integration via IKineticTarget (3 Archetypes: Swarm, Turret, Brute).
  • One dedicated Prototype Lab scene for physics & combat testing.
  • A progression of 5 to 7 compact puzzle & encounter rooms.
  • Basic UI: Crosshair, hand status icons, slow-motion meter, pause menu.
  • Simple audio cues: wind blast, suction hum, slow-mo chime, impact thuds.

Strictly Out of Scope (Forbidden / Cut)

  • No Complex Gun Inventories: No ammo counters, weapon wheels, or conventional firearms (air powers only).
  • No Story Cutscenes: No character dialogue, voice actors, or scripted cinematics.
  • No Open World: No sprawling maps, terrain systems, or outdoor hubs.
  • No Complex Destruction: No mesh fracturing or real-time breaking glass.
  • No Inventory or Crafting: No bag management or item combining.
  • No Multiplayer: Single-player offline only.
  • No Character Customization: No skill trees, stat upgrades, or skins.
